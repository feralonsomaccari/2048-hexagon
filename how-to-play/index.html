How to play 2048 Hexagon

This guide covers the two board sizes, the power-ups on the Small board, strategy written for a hexagonal grid, the achievements and the leaderboard. If you have not played yet, the rules are short: slide the board in one of six directions, and merge tiles with the same number.

Play 2048 Hexagon

Board sizes

Choose a board when you start a new game.

Board Cells Power-ups Goal
Small 7 Yes 2048
Big 19 No 2048

The Small board is the harder puzzle. Seven cells fill up fast, so it gives you power-ups to stay alive. The Big board has room to breathe and no power-ups at all. On the Big board, tiles often spawn two at a time, which keeps the pressure on even with 19 cells.

Power-ups on the Small board

The Small board gives you a fixed number of charges. Charges do not refill during a game.

  • Undo (3 charges): step back one move.
  • Swap (2 charges): exchange the position of two tiles.
  • Freeze (2 charges): skip the next tile spawn, so you get a free move.
  • Double (2 charges): double the value of one tile, up to 512.

There is a reason to save them. Every power-up you finish the game without using adds three percent to your final score. A clean run is worth more than a rescued one.

If the board locks and you still have an undo charge, you can revive: the undo charge rolls the board back one move and the game continues.

Strategy for a hexagonal board

Advice written for square 2048 only half works here. These points are specific to the hex grid.

Pick a corner and never leave it

The classic 2048 rule still holds, and it matters more on a hexagon. Choose one corner, keep your largest tile there, and use only the moves that leave it in place. On a hexagon each corner is held by two of the six directions, so you have more safe moves than on a square board, but also more ways to break the pattern by accident.

Six directions cut both ways

More directions mean more merges available on any given board. They also mean the board rearranges more violently. A move that looks harmless can pull a tile out of your corner from a direction you were not watching. Before each move, check what leaves the corner, not only what merges.

Build along a line, not in a cluster

Merges happen between neighbours in a straight line. A tidy descending line of 256, 128, 64, 32 collapses in one move when the top of the line merges. A cluster of similar values with no order needs several moves to resolve, and each move spawns another tile.

Chain merges score more

Several pairs can merge in a single move. Setting up a chain of four or more is worth far more than four separate merges, and it clears space faster. The Combo Artist achievement asks for exactly this.

Freeze is a planning tool

Most players keep Freeze for emergencies. It is stronger used early: skipping a spawn gives you one move with no new tile, which is often enough to reorder a line before the board gets tight.

Achievements

The game tracks 15 achievements. Some ask for a tile: 512, 1024, 2048, 4096 and 8192, counted separately on each board. Others ask for a way of playing: score 5,000 or 20,000 points in one game, merge four pairs in a single move, merge on 12 or 16 moves in a row, win without touching a power-up, or win after a revive. Unlock every one of them and the Completionist achievement follows.

Leaderboard

Beat a high score and you can add your name to the public leaderboard. The board keeps the top five scores for each board size and shows the number of moves each run took. The entry is optional, and the name is yours to choose. The game asks for nothing else.

About the game

2048 Hexagon is based on 2048 by Gabriele Cirulli, which was itself inspired by 1024 and Threes. This version is built with React and TypeScript and runs entirely in your browser. The source code is open on GitHub.
